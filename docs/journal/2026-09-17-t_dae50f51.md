# The MCP dispatcher surface: the verb-level claim-FTS lane repair moves into the handlers — REVISE was the one live member of the class there

## Intent

Close the gap `t_a6bf30a8` carded after its catch-up repair: `src/index.ts` is a **second dispatcher
surface** — an MCP server whose `server.tool(...)` callbacks call the protocol handlers **directly**,
not through the `SmartwareCore` wrappers — so the survey's *verb-level* lane repairs (which lived in
the wrappers) never reached it. The card asked: probe first (RED at the current tip), decide per verb
whether the repairs belong in the handlers or the wrappers, one reviewable PR, a regression pin for at
least CORRECT over the MCP surface, the full VERIFY.md gate, and an answer for how a future verb
cannot silently regress (the class has now been found three times: two wrapper-level follow-ups, this
surface).

A reviewer measurement attached to the card had already shown the headline instance — `smartware_correct`
answering `[]` over MCP — was **fixed by the catch-up repair** (the correction's own scope is one of
the scopes `replayCatchUp`'s report covers). The card's own text demanded re-measurement rather than
taking that as settled, so this lane measured before it edited.

## What was measured first (out of tree, three builds, same probe bytes)

New instrument `scripts/mcp-surface-probe.mjs` (four scenarios; `node scripts/mcp-surface-probe.mjs
<dist-dir> all <out.jsonl>`). Each scenario drives the **exact call shape** the corresponding
`src/index.ts` tool uses — no `SmartwareCore` wrapper — against a brain whose claims are in the lane,
then compares the live process's recall with a **fresh open** of the same brain:

| scenario | fd55cc2 (pre-`t_a6bf30a8`) | 63b608f (this branch's base) | this lane's tip |
|---|---|---|---|
| `correct-changed` (target in lane) | in-process `[]` vs fresh `[replacement, target]` | in-process `[replacement]` vs fresh `[replacement, target]` | unchanged from base |
| `correct-wrong` (target retracted) | in-process `[]` vs fresh `[replacement]` | in-process `[replacement]` == fresh | **equal** |
| `revise` (MCP call shape) | in-process `[replacement]` vs fresh `[replacement, revised]` | same | **equal** |
| `revise-core` (`SmartwareCore.revise`) | same | same | **equal** |

Two of those rows are the whole card:

- **CORRECT over the MCP surface is green at the base.** The catch-up repair covers the corrected
  scope, on this surface too — re-measured, not taken from the card. The remaining live-vs-fresh
  difference on the `changed` shape is the **demotion resurrection** (`e5f093e`/`3ae8a6b`, in
  `integration/v0.8.0`, **not** in this ancestry): a fresh open erases the store-side supersession of
  the corrected claim; nothing about the lane. The pin therefore uses the demotion-free `wrong` shape
  (see below) — the reviewer's methodological note, honoured.
- **REVISE is the one live member at the tip, on both surfaces.** On this base PR #29's wrapper-level
  fix is not in the ancestry, so REVISE re-synced nowhere: the process that performed a warranted
  revision answered `[replacement]` while every restart served `[replacement, revised]` — RED on the
  MCP call shape (the card's sweep) *and* on `SmartwareCore.revise` (the wrapper).

`scripts/mcp-stdio-probe.mjs` then drives the **real transport**: it seeds the same composition, spawns
`dist/index.js` as an MCP server over stdio, calls `smartware_revise` and `smartware_recall`, stops the
server, and opens the brain fresh. Pre-fix (63b608f build): the server's own recall answered
`[replacement]` while the fresh open answered `[replacement, revised]`, and the lane row for the
revised claim was absent (`lane_after == lane_before`). Post-fix: lane rebuilt, `in_process == fresh`,
`in_process_serves_revised: true`. That pair is the end-to-end proof that the **wiring** in
`src/index.ts` passes the lane, not only that the call shape would work.

## The sweep (per verb, `src/index.ts`'s twenty tools)

Enumeration by the store-write inventory of the handlers (`grep insertClaim|syncFromJsonlVersion|

- **no claim rows at all** — `context`, `recall`, `query`, `read`, `explain`, `export_scope`, `grant`,

- **`compile` / `reflect`** — `handleCompile(..., searchIndex, ...)`; the compiler's stage 4.5 re-sync

- **`correct`** — covered by the handler catch-up repair; re-measured here over the MCP call shape.
  (The core wrapper's extra scoped re-sync is now redundant with the catch-up report; left alone —
  it is semantics-neutral, and removing it is not this card's question.)
- **`revise`** — **the gap; fixed here.**
- **`forget`** — handler catch-up repair + the tool's own raw-index reconcile (`reconcileObservationIndexRow`),
  same shape as the core wrapper. Pinned by the catch-up lane's FORGET case.
- **`forget_scope`** — the handler owns its lane work and receives `searchIndex` through its deps; the
  survey verified it and `v050-rebuild-forget-provenance.test.ts` pins the every-lane-zero property.
- **`quarantine_review`** — handler catch-up repair + the tool's raw-index reconcile. Pinned by the
  catch-up lane's quarantine case.

So the sweep found exactly **one** live member (`revise`) — and confirmed, by measurement, that the
card's headline instance was already closed by the parent lane.

## Decision: the repairs live in the handlers (the card asked for a decision, not a choice)

`handleRevise` (`src/protocol/revise.ts`) now takes the caller's lane as a trailing optional
`searchIndex` and re-syncs the revised claim's scope on **every path that syncs a claim row**
(`store.syncFromJsonlVersion` in the main path *and* in the idempotent replay of an already-committed
operation — the two paths where a claim version reaches the store). Both dispatchers pass their lane:
`SmartwareCore.revise` and `src/index.ts`'s `smartware_revise` tool.

Why handlers, stated plainly: a wrapper-level repair **provably does not reach this surface** — REVISE
was broken on the MCP call shape *and* (on this base) on the wrapper, so a fix in either wrapper alone
would have left the other broken; and the catch-up class had already chosen the same placement
(`t_a6bf30a8`). The dispatcher that owns the lane hands it over; the layer that writes the rows does
the repair — layer 1 still owns no lane, and the handler calls layer 3's scoped sync exactly as the
catch-up repair does. A dispatcher that passes no lane behaves exactly as before.

The relationship to PR #29 (REVISE's wrapper fix, not in this ancestry): when both land, PR #29's
post-handler `syncSearchFromClaims` in `core.revise` becomes a **redundant second rebuild** of the
same scope — semantics-neutral, but the integrator should keep the handler's, because it is the one
the MCP surface gets too. This branch's `core.revise` passes the lane so the wrapper surface is
correct on *this* base as well.

## The guard against the next verb (the card's question 4)

Two layers, both executable:

1. **The placement itself.** The repair sits where the rows are written, so a dispatcher cannot
   forget it and the two known dispatchers cannot drift apart. The remaining failure mode — a *new*
   verb bypassing the repair — is what (2) is for.
2. **A classification tripwire in the pin** (`test/protocol/mcp-surface-lane-sync.test.ts`, last
   describe): it reads `src/index.ts`, extracts every `server.tool('…')` registration, and requires
   (a) every registered tool to be classified in an in-file registry (`claim-rows` or `none`),
   (b) every `claim-rows` tool's **registration slice to mention `searchIndex`** (wiring tripwire —
   it fails at 63b608f on `smartware_revise` and at fd55cc2 on `smartware_correct` too), and
   (c) every `claim-rows` tool to name where its lane behaviour is pinned. A new tool added to
   `src/index.ts` fails the suite until a human classifies it and points at its pin.

Honest limits, stated so they are not discovered as surprises: the tripwire enforces *classification
and wiring text*, not the correctness of a classification; a handler that receives a lane and does not
use it would still pass — that remains a review-time question, and this entry is where the class's
membership is recorded. The reachability of the class is unchanged (multi-process/multi-writer, or a
single process taking a verb whose repair its dispatcher does not pass — which is now a failing test).

  the wrapper's). Semantics-neutral, but keep one — the handler's.
- **The `correct-changed` live-vs-fresh difference** is the demotion resurrection on the fresh leg
  (`e5f093e`/`3ae8a6b`), carried in the local-only `integration/v0.8.0` and not on this base. On a
  demotion-durable tree the two legs should agree; re-run `node scripts/mcp-surface-probe.mjs <dist>
  correct-changed` there (same re-run obligation `t_a6bf30a8` recorded for its arm 2).
- **Not measured:** whether any other *dispatcher* exists that calls claim-row-moving handlers without

  wrapper-surface ones). The tripwire covers `src/index.ts` only; extending it to `src/mcp.ts`'s
  registrations is cheap and left as a named option, not a silent gap.
- **Not proven:** independent verification of this lane — the evidence is producer-run. This change
  touches no released schema, but the reviewer may want an execution-lens round on the tripwire's
  extraction logic (it parses source text, and that is the part most likely to rot).
